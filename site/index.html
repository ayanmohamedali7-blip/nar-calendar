How it works

1. Two satellites, two rulers

MODIS (on Terra and Aqua) has mapped fire since November 2000 with 1 km pixels. VIIRS (on Suomi NPP) started in January 2012 with 375 m pixels and sees many small fires that MODIS misses. Suomi NPP itself stopped delivering data on 2 November 2026, so the same problem is about to return with its successors. Put side by side, the two records look like a jump in fire in 2012 that never happened.

2. Keep only real fires

We keep detections that FIRMS labels as presumed vegetation fire, drop low-confidence ones, then mask every 1 km cell around a known static heat source (gas flares, industry) and every place that is hot all year round. In Libya this removes about 98 % of all hot spots.

3. One common unit

Both sensors are placed on the same 0.01° grid (about 1 km). A cell that burns on a given day counts once, whatever the pixel size: a burning cell-day. When a satellite was out of service for some days, the month is scaled up to a full month and marked.

4. Learn the link where both fly

From 2012 to 2026 both sensors watched the same land. For your area we rank all those months twice, once by MODIS and once by VIIRS, and match the ranks: the 10th busiest MODIS month takes the value of the 10th busiest VIIRS month. This is quantile mapping, the technique climate scientists use to join instrument records.

5. One series, with honest error bars

Before 2012 the calendar shows MODIS converted to VIIRS units; from 2012 it shows VIIRS itself. Each overlap year is hidden in turn and predicted from MODIS alone, so the error you see is measured, not assumed. We tested three methods this way and kept the one that is both accurate and keeps the real ups and downs.

Limits

Satellites miss fires under cloud and between overpasses. A month where MODIS saw nothing gets the typical small-fire level VIIRS sees in such months, which assumes the years before 2012 resembled 2012 to 2026. The newest months are near-real-time data and are marked as preliminary. A single early month is a rough estimate; yearly totals and the season shape are more reliable.
